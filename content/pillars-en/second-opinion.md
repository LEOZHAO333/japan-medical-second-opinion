## Short answer

A Japanese specialist second opinion is a written opinion from a relevant Japanese specialist on diagnosis, staging and treatment options, based on your complete records (pathology, imaging, treatment history). It is most useful at key decision points: an uncertain diagnosis, disagreement over treatment, poor response to treatment, or uncertainty about next steps after recurrence. It is decision support, not a transfer of care, and it does not guarantee a change to the original plan.

## 1. When a second opinion is worth considering

- **At diagnosis**: atypical pathology, uncertain staging, or different hospitals giving different diagnoses.
- **When choosing treatment**: several options are offered, or the proposed treatment carries high risk (extensive surgery, possible loss of organ function).
- **When treatment is not going well**: poor response, resistance or recurrence, with uncertainty about switching drugs, surgery, radiotherapy or a clinical trial.
- **When special technologies come up**: wanting to know whether BNCT, proton therapy or new drugs are worth considering, starting with whether you fall within the approved scope.

If there is already a clear standard plan and good communication with your treating doctor, a second opinion is not necessarily required.

## 2. What a second opinion can and cannot do

**It can:**
- re-examine diagnosis, staging and the treatment plan from the perspective of a Japanese specialist;
- point out gaps in the existing records and tests that may still be needed;
- compare possible treatment paths with their assumptions and risks;
- judge whether a special treatment such as BNCT is worth a formal evaluation request.

**It cannot:**
- replace your treating doctor or prescribe;
- guarantee that a treatment will work or that a hospital will accept you;
- reach firm conclusions when records are incomplete.

## 3. Records to prepare

1. **Pathology**: reports including immunohistochemistry and genetic testing; slide review may be needed.
2. **Imaging**: recent CT, MRI or PET-CT as original DICOM files, ideally with before-and-after comparison.
3. **Treatment history**: operation notes, regimens and dates of chemotherapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy, radiotherapy plans and doses.
4. **Current status**: recent blood tests, performance status, main symptoms and current medications.
5. **Your questions**: the two or three questions you most want answered, such as whether surgery is needed or how to choose the next line of treatment.

Record preparation is the key to the quality of a second opinion.

## 4. Process and timeline

1. **Record check and initial conversation**: confirm the stage of diagnosis, treatment history and main questions.
2. **Organisation and translation**: arrange the timeline and key records the way Japanese specialists read them.
3. **Specialist review**: records are matched to relevant specialists for review.
4. **Written opinion**: issued 10 to 15 working days after complete records are received.
5. **Discussion with your treating doctor**: take the written opinion back to your local team to decide next steps.

Fee: CNY 15,000 per case, including the Japanese hospital consultation fee, with no additional charge.

## 5. Common misunderstandings

- **"A second opinion means going to Japan for treatment."** No. In most cases its value is in helping you make a better decision where you are.
- **"Just find the most famous doctor."** The right specialty and complete records matter more.
- **"Fewer records make it faster."** Incomplete records often lead to conditional conclusions and back-and-forth requests.
- **"A different opinion means the original plan was wrong."** Different opinions often reflect different assumptions and trade-offs and should be discussed with your treating doctor.
