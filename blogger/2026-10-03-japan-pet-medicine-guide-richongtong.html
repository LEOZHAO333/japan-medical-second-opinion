Japanese Pet Medicines: 6 Checks Every Pet Owner Should Make

Published and last reviewed: October 3, 2026 | Educational content

Direct answer

Do not judge a Japanese veterinary medicine by its brand name, country of origin, or online reviews alone. Before discussing a product with a veterinarian, verify six points: the animal species, active ingredient, dosage form and strength, officially approved use in Japan, contraindications and interactions, and whether a veterinary prescription or follow-up monitoring is required.

Richongtong (日宠通) translates and organizes official Japanese pet-health information for Chinese-speaking pet owners. It is an information and education service, not an online veterinary clinic or a medicine retailer.

Why a familiar drug name is not enough

One Chinese translation may be used for several products. Similar Japanese brand names may contain different ingredients or strengths. A product used in dogs may be unsafe for cats, and an indication approved in Japan cannot automatically be applied to another country, another species, or another disease.

  1. Species: Confirm whether the product is intended for dogs, cats, or another animal.
  2. Active ingredient: Do not assume that similar brand names are interchangeable.
  3. Dosage form and strength: Tablets, drops, injections, and topical products require different handling and dosing.
  4. Approved use: Check the official Japanese indication, target population, and use conditions.
  5. Contraindications and interactions: Age, body weight, liver or kidney function, and concurrent medicines can change the risk.
  6. Prescription and monitoring: Some medicines require a veterinarian’s prescription, laboratory testing, or imaging follow-up.

Japan’s National Veterinary Assay Laboratory maintains an official database for veterinary medicinal products. It can be searched by product name, ingredient, marketing authorization holder, approved effect, directions for use, and regulatory category. Richongtong gives priority to this type of primary source instead of relying on e-commerce descriptions, anecdotal experiences, or human-medicine labels.

When should a pet receive urgent veterinary care?

Seek urgent veterinary care if a pet has difficulty breathing, repeated seizures, altered consciousness, inability to urinate, repeated vomiting with signs of dehydration, suspected toxin exposure, severe trauma, or a rapidly worsening condition. Do not wait for an online reply and do not experiment with an unfamiliar medicine.

For chronic or complex conditions, prepare the diagnosis, recent tests, body-weight and symptom changes, current and previous medicines, reasons for stopping treatment, adverse effects, and the three questions you most want the veterinarian to answer. Better-organized information usually leads to a more useful consultation.

Frequently asked questions

Are Japanese veterinary medicines always better?

No. Suitability depends on the diagnosis, active ingredient, approved indication, dose, monitoring requirements, and the individual animal—not the country printed on the package.

Can a human medicine be given directly to a dog or cat?

Do not do this without veterinary guidance. Some human medicines are toxic to dogs or cats. Even when an ingredient is also used in veterinary care, the formulation and dose may be different.

Can Richongtong diagnose a pet or issue a prescription online?

No. Richongtong provides source-based information and health education. It does not replace a veterinary hospital or licensed prescriber.

What should I check first when researching a Japanese pet medicine?

Start with the exact product name, active ingredient, species, dosage form and strength, and officially approved Japanese use. Then check contraindications, interactions, prescription status, and monitoring requirements.
